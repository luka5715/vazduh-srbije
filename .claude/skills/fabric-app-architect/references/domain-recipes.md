# Domain starting points

Use these as question patterns, not as claims that measures or columns exist. Confirm the model before naming bindings. Select only the pages needed for the user's decisions.

| Domain | Overview decision | Analysis | Detail/action | Metric risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sales | Are revenue and margin on track? | Trend, product/customer drivers, target variance | Customer/product exceptions | Revenue vs invoicing date, returns, weighted margin |
| Procurement | Where do cost and supplier risk need attention? | Spend concentration, price change, lead-time distribution | Supplier/order exceptions | Savings baseline, currency, partial deliveries |
| HR | How is the workforce changing? | Department/shift mix, hires/exits, tenure cohorts | Restricted workforce detail | Snapshot vs event grain, distinct people, sensitive fields |
| Jira/delivery | Where is flow blocked? | Throughput, cycle-time distribution, aging work | Issue drillthrough and blocker list | Reopened work, status history, business/calendar time |
| Finance | What drives variance and liquidity? | Actual/plan/prior, account/entity contributions | Reconciliation and account detail | Semi-additive balances, signs, FX and fiscal periods |

Prefer distributions and medians alongside averages when skew matters. Avoid ranking employee performance from convenient proxy metrics. Treat an operational action as a supported workflow with an owner and backend, not a decorative button.

## Build a recognizable product

Choose a domain-specific title, meaningful default period and stable status vocabulary. Carry the same metric names and colors from overview to detail. Make the next investigation visible; do not add a page of unrelated charts to make the app seem larger.
